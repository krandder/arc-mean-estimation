# ARC's matching-sampling bar

**True now (checked 2026-10-31; `inferred`: the opus-mats assistant's answers to Kelvin plus two subagent reports it relayed, `arc-random-networks-search-2026-10-26.md` and `careful-read-wind-tunnels-tabpfn-algzoo-2026-10-26.md` in the mats outbox; this wiki opened none of the sources).** ARC defines an explanation of a model as "a mechanistic estimate of the model's loss". The bar is to estimate that loss (here accuracy) at least as well as random sampling, in mean squared error per unit of compute, with no sampling of the model. The bar is a curve over all compute budgets, not a single N. It has been beaten with a guarantee only for tiny trained models (exactly, by geometry) and for wide, shallow, random ReLU MLPs (by cumulant propagation); trained networks, attention, depth and narrow widths are open. Our own test of training a transformer to be easy to estimate cut the propagation error by 9-191× at a small accuracy cost, yet cumulant propagation still lost to sampling by 15-50× (section on making propagation easy). Kelvin has parked the estimability track. His note for later: a loss that makes activations more Gaussian is not the way to go; making activations behave like "symbolic activations" that follow known rules, by some expectation-maximization analogy, is probably more useful. The method needs a named reference input distribution, so it answers "average or rare behaviour under a distribution we can name", never "what does this network do on an unknown input". For our small PFNs ([[pfn-deduplication-and-wind-tunnel-mechanisms]]) the agreed target is a mechanistic account of where a trained network departs from exact Bayes, which must beat both a stored answer and the Bayes baseline itself; a partial, causally tested mechanism is the realistic first step and the bar a stretch goal. The first attempt on our tiny PFN did not meet the bar (section "First test on a trained PFN").

## Two kinds of randomness

1. **Random inputs define the quantity.** The loss is an average over the input distribution; for the 432-parameter 2nd-argmax RNN M₁₆,₁₀ (95.3% accurate) inputs are 10 standard-Gaussian numbers. Sampling with N draws has MSE p(1−p)/N, about 0.045/N (N = 1,000: standard error about 0.7 points; 10⁶: about 0.02 points), and one sample costs about 10⁴ FLOPs for this model.
2. **Random models define the estimator's error.** One fixed model gives a deterministic estimate that is either right or off, so ARC averages over noisy-weight copies (cheap) or training seeds (better, dearer). Needed only once an estimate is approximate: "an estimate for a single model could be close by chance".

For our setting: inputs are episodes from our prior, the quantity is e.g. a model's mean KL to exact Bayes over duplicate-count episodes, and the models are perturbed or reseeded tiny deduplication networks.

## What has actually beaten sampling

- **M₂,₂ (10 parameters, error about 1 in 13,000).** The ReLU, no-bias structure makes the logit difference piecewise linear with pieces cut by rays through the origin; after rescaling, Δ = a₀x₀ − a₁x₁ on every piece, and only in two thin regions where a₀ ≈ a₁ to about 1 part in 2¹¹ does the boundary miss x₀ = x₁. The error rate is an angular fraction, about 1/(2π·2¹¹), a handful of operations, and could be exact to floating point. Sampling needs tens of thousands of runs just to see one error. No weight perturbation is reported (nothing left to average).
- **M₄,₃ (32 parameters):** approximate symmetries only "roughly match" sampling, with the noisy-weights footnote; no guarantee at larger budgets, since any bias b caps the MSE at b² while sampling keeps falling as 1/N.
- **M₁₆,₁₀ (432 parameters):** feature subcircuits found, no estimate beating sampling; the open challenge. ARC fully understands models only up to 32 parameters; one unreviewed third-party repo claims about 2× better than sampling at around a million runs. The AlgZoo set is RNNs for 2nd argmax, argmedian and median plus transformers for longest cycle, 8 to 1,408 parameters.
- **No guarantee in general.** The matching-sampling principle is a stated conjecture (some mechanistic procedure with suitable advice matches sampling at every budget). The assistant's own aside, not ARC's: use the estimate as a control variate for sampling, never worse than sampling but not purely mechanistic.

Our own MATS lane results against this bar are on [[mats-noisy-channel-interp]], which reports the lane's AlgZoo and deep-MLP tests. On the AlgZoo side (relayed, 2026-10-29): noisy RNNs twice as wide match ARC's model, yet under ARC's protocol the deductive estimates lose to sampling by 10^3-10^5 (M_{16,10}: about 10^5), while on M_{2,2} both beat sampling by 10^4-10^6; descriptions fitted to the same model predict its error rate to 3-6% but are circular under ARC's rules; with a little sampling added they still lose by 2.5-85x once setup is counted, a deductive estimate would need 3-5% relative accuracy near the decision thresholds to match sampling, and the network's errors are its departures from its own algorithm (reading the answer off the algorithmic part errs 1% against the network's 4%); on the ARC side, a hybrid of ARC's estimate and a sampler beats the best sampler 1.2-10x at depth 8 but nothing wins at depth 32 (finished 2026-10-30, relayed); ARC's agenda and its London stream are on [[arc-mechanistic-estimation-robinson]].

### How ARC scores an AlgZoo explanation, and what our lane may regenerate

Checked 2026-11-02 (`inferred`: the opus-mats assistant's reading of ARC's AlgZoo post and "Competing with sampling", answering Kelvin; not re-opened here). Kelvin's question was whether the criterion is only "predict the loss better than sampling". Essentially yes:

- **The task.** For M₁₆,₁₀: "mechanistically estimating the accuracy … that matches the performance of random sampling in terms of mean squared error versus compute". ARC call a mechanistic estimate of the loss an explanation, the estimate is deductive (no sampling), and *matching*, not beating, sampling on M₁₆,₁₀ would already be "a major research breakthrough".
- **Where the MSE comes from.** One model gives one number, so the MSE is taken over versions of the model with noise added to the weights (enough to change its accuracy) or over training seeds.
- **Per training seed, regenerating the structure counts.** In ARC's formal version each trained model comes with its own explanation ("advice"), found alongside training with at most training's compute; it may be as large as the network and need not be verifiable. Only the final estimate must be deductive, and ARC do not say whether finding the explanation may sample. So a structure search per seed is allowed if its compute is reported.
- **Per noisy-weight version, it does not count as a free explanation.** The weight noise is the randomness the explanation may not see, otherwise it could write the accuracy down. Find the structure once per trained model and recompute only the deductive estimate per noisy version; regenerating per version is allowed only inside the estimator, deductively and with its compute counted. A sampling step there moves the method to the "little sampling" column.
- **Our lane's protocol (Kelvin, same session).** Report three error-against-compute columns: deductive only, deductive plus a little sampling, and plain sampling. The first per-input search (each input right or wrong as the network, at most 1% differing) was stricter than the bar, so the AlgZoo agent was told to score the structure on the accuracy estimate, loss second, under ARC's protocol with MSE over weight-noise versions of our noisy model; the per-input structure stays useful for "which inputs fail and why". The loss-scored search must also match the network's average loss and error rate within sampling's error at the same compute, and within several held-out input groups fixed in advance (for example by the gap between the top two values), to catch a right average for the wrong reason. Results so far are on [[mats-noisy-channel-interp-history]] (AlgZoo lane).

**Surprise accounting, ARC's "full understanding" bar.** Total surprise = the bits of the facts about the weights the explanation relies on (signs, which coefficient is larger, near-equalities) plus the leftover surprise of the model's actual accuracy given the estimate; full understanding means the total is about the bits of optimisation that selected the model, at most the bits in the weights. ARC's example M₂,₂ (10 parameters): facts cost 14 + 4 + 22 bits, the 22 being two coefficients equal to 1 part in 2¹¹, about 0 left over, about 40 in total, which plausibly matches the selection; M₄,₃ is "more or less" understood and nothing larger. ARC call this bar harder and work on error against compute. It is a description-length criterion like ours, with three differences: ARC count bits about the weights against a prior over weights, we count bits about the activations against the noise; without noise there is no natural precision, so ARC price a near-equality by the precision their calculation happened to need while with noise σ sets the scale; and for ARC the bits only judge full understanding at the end, while for us they are measured and added at every layer. An open, unworked idea registered in `outbox/pending-discussion-kelvin.md`: price each fact only to the precision at which noise lets it change behaviour (possibly capped near log₂(1/σ) bits per fact; ARC's 22-bit equality is about the precision of our 0.1%-noise model) and use our divergence bits as the leftover-surprise term.

**Median RNN size.** One-layer ReLU RNN, no biases, h_{t+1} = ReLU(W_hh h_t + W_hi x_t) read over the Gaussian inputs, one linear readout of the final state; parameters h² + 2h, so hidden 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 give 8, 24, 80, 288, 1,088, at sequence lengths 2, 3, 5, 10. Length 2 is trivial (the median is the mean); length 3 is the first real case, and hidden 4-8 at length 3-5 (24-80 parameters) would sit just above the largest understood model. The assistant had read the code but not the RMSE values (they are in a figure it did not extract).

**Which families matter to us (assistant's ranking; Kelvin: "That we might think are interesting").** Median first: it aggregates noisy numbers into one estimate whose answer does not depend on order while the RNN reads in a fixed order ("aggregation through fixed-order processing" in miniature), and it is a regression like our Gaussian prediction, so error against the exact answer plays the role of our deviation from Bayes. Longest cycle second (small in-pass search by pointer-following, far from aggregation). 2nd argmax M₁₆,₁₀ third: the result people would recognise, with ARC's stated bar of "a major research breakthrough", but an order-statistics problem on which several groups have failed. All of these compute a known deterministic function, while ours are judged against exact Bayes, which gives the tiny network ([[pfn-tiny-reliability-world]]) a reference algorithm that AlgZoo models lack; median is the natural outside check, since a method that explains our network can then be tried on an unexplained zoo model.

## Random, untrained networks

"Estimating the expected output of wide random MLPs more efficiently than sampling" (Wu, Lecomte, Winer, Robinson, Hilton, Christiano; arXiv 2605.05179, May 2026) takes one random ReLU MLP (weights N(0, 2/n)) and estimates its expected output over Gaussian inputs without sampling, by cumulant propagation to order K with Hermite expansions.

- **Theory:** at fixed depth, error ε in O(n/ε²) time against Θ(n²/ε²) for Monte Carlo, a factor of the width n.
- **Experiments:** at width 256 and 4 hidden layers it beats Monte Carlo on MSE per FLOP over 7 orders of magnitude of compute, sometimes by over 100× (about 1,000× at 2 hidden layers), and does much better on low probabilities.
- **Our own scored test at 256 x 32 (2 November, `inferred`, relayed, report pending):** ARC's second-order method beats quasi-random sampling only at small budgets (about 2x at roughly 450 forward passes at precision near 1e-3; a referee on 2026-11-03 added that means-only propagation, order 1 at one pass, beats it 16-90x at precision 1-4e-2 against extrapolated sampling, so the win is in two windows, not one); at 65,000 passes sampling is 7-40x more accurate (median 22x), third order loses 5-30x, and a radius-fix variant floors at 1.4-2.3e-4, which sampling reaches in 3,000-5,000 passes. The fix's window does not grow with width (floor falling like n^(-0.67); plain sampling needs only 18-30x its cost to match the floor at width 256, quasi-random 7-12x), and through all layers it is no clear gain over ARC's second-order variant (5x better to 3x worse per net). The review found its cost mis-counted (about 400 passes charged for a recursion that used about 500,000 samples per layer), so the report dropped any gain claim: as run it costs at least 255x plain sampling, and a sample-free version could win only with fewer than about five second-order steps per layer. The lane's final report (2 November) is negative only for the methods tested (moment closures of orders 2-4 and the radius fix): if ARC's own scaling law is a lower bound for every order, the best error falls like compute^(-gamma) with gamma going to 0 as depth grows with width, 0.375 at 256 x 32 against sampling's 1 (measured 0.52 at width 32 to 0.30 at 256). An adversarial review judged it a benchmark for ARC's next method, not a proof that none can win, and the report was reframed that way. Two untested classes were then run (relayed, 2 November, trial signal only, not yet scored): the public "scale mode" proposal claiming convergence at depth proportional to width (ARC challenge forum topic 18298) is essentially the radius-corrected closure, beats quasi-random sampling only 1.2-2.6x with a gain per order that falls with width (exponent 1.2, 0.68, 0.28 at widths 32, 64, 128), and its author's own repository concedes the margin shrinks; the first registered results agree (at 256 x 32 a median error of 3.6e-4 at about 700 passes, 2.5x better than quasi-random sampling there but matched by it at about 2.3x the cost, third order losing 4-20x, gain per order 0.30 against ARC's 0.32; on the author's own 64 x 16 nets it loses to quasi-random sampling on 7 of 8, median 0.79x); the Phase 2 "covariance propagation beats sampling" claim is ARC's second-order closure at 1024 x 16, a very shallow net, where it is 6x more accurate than quasi-random sampling at equal compute and loses only to sampling with the full budget (by 6x in raw error). Future-aware methods (keeping the directions later layers need exact) gain 4.8x at width 64 but 1.7x at 256 for 16 directions (a later interim run gives 5.4x, 3.2x, 2.2x at widths 64, 128, 256), the number of directions that matter grows in proportion to width (proved by gpt-6-astra in the mean-field model), the best equal-cost hybrid gain is 0.8-1.1x, and their large oracle gains are benchmarks, not methods. Full results and objections on [[mats-noisy-channel-interp]], "Overnight theory lanes". The scale-mode test is now fully scored (relayed, 2 November): neither registered escape criterion was met; on its author's depth n/4 nets its exponent is about 0.1 and it beats quasi-random sampling at only 2 of 5 widths, and on the shallow 1024 x 16 shape it does scale (exponent 0.83), so the negative holds when depth grows with width and not for shallow nets. Kelvin then turned the lanes to the sampling side, asking for anything that might estimate the mean at a better asymptote per precision (randomness and pre-integration lanes; so far structured sampling gains only a constant factor (scored best 2.9x at ARC's budget, under a proven ceiling of about 10-19x for weight-blind averaging estimators), with results and the unexplained Phase-1 board entry on [[sampling-side-asymptote-lanes]]; framing on [[noisy-stack-bit-budget-model]], "Beyond bits"). The scale-mode report is filed (`outbox/scale-mode-2026-11-02.md`) with its verdict unchanged after outside review, and the lane's last two runs are in the ARC-update report: ARC's most expensive third-order variant at 256 x 32 on one net is worse and costlier than the plain one.
- **Breaks:** at 8 hidden layers it starts to underperform sampling once K = 4; the conjectured error grows like c_K(L/n)^K (a heuristic analysis; MSE ∝ L^K at width 256). Narrow networks are open; wall-clock it is slower than sampling.
- **Why ARC's ladder is slow: per-step error summed over depth, not amplification (checked 2026-11-03; `inferred`, relayed from the cumulant-ambiguity lane's report, measured on 2²⁰ Gaussian inputs, median of 3 nets; the mechanism is heuristic).** On the true layer distributions the per-step closure error of the next-layer mean is about c_K(l/n)/n (l the layer index), so n × per-step error is about the same at n = 32, 64, 128 when l/n = 1/8 (about 0.02 for order 2, 0.003–0.005 for order 3, 3–6×10⁻⁴ for order 4). Its sum over L = n/8 steps is therefore width-independent: about 1.6×10⁻³, 2.5×10⁻⁴ and 2.3×10⁻⁵ for orders 2, 3, 4, against ARC's ladder of about 1×10⁻³, 1.4–2.5×10⁻⁴ and 3.4×10⁻⁵ (n = 32). A cut experiment (start ARC's recursion from the true cumulants at layer c and attribute the final error per step) shows that at depth n/8 the full recursion is 1.0–2.2x that sum (64×8, order 2: 2.0×10⁻³ against a 1.6×10⁻³ sum; order 3: 2.3×10⁻⁴ against 2.6×10⁻⁴; 128×16 alike), so **nothing amplifies there**. This refutes the earlier working explanation (early-layer higher-order errors amplified downstream) for ARC's shape. Amplification appears only when depth is large against width: at depth n/4 and beyond the recursion is 5–300x the sum (32×8 order 3: 6.3×10⁻³ against 1.3×10⁻³), and order 3 diverges at 16×8. The ladder is flat-ish because each extra order divides the per-step error by a roughly width-independent factor (about 6–8 for 2→3, about 10 for 3→4) while costing about n times more compute (the arc-update lane's measure), so the precision exponent behaves like log(factor)/log(n) and falls toward zero with width. Max-entropy closure at order 4 is 2–3x worse than Edgeworth; order-4 values at n ≥ 64 are near the 2²⁰-sample noise floor; ARC's own 2→3 ratio worsens with width (0.12 to 0.24 over n = 32 to 256) while the 1D ratio stays 0.13–0.18, perhaps from its factored variants (unchecked); an order-4 cut at 64×8 was still running. The worst-case side of the same lane and the open lower-bound theorem are on [[arc-pseudorandom-code-conjecture]], target 1.
- **Why depth hurts:** each nonlinearity creates fresh non-Gaussianity of size about 1/n, earlier truncation error is mixed by later layers, so after L layers the departure from Gaussian is about L/n. Sampling does not degrade with depth. The L/n expansion parameter is a known theme of Roberts, Yaida and Hanin (2022), which the assistant cited from memory. ARC writes that it believes a sample-free algorithm whose error does not grow with depth can be developed, left to future work. The assistant's speculative routes: track only the statistics that matter (ARC's L² sketching), re-anchor around a non-Gaussian family, or hybridise with sampling.
- **Related ARC items:** "Competing with sampling" (2025-11-18) states the principle with random MLPs as an instance and leaves random RNNs, CNNs and transformers open; "Expectations of random products" (2026-05-15) covers halfspaces, #3-SAT and permanents; the White-Box Estimation Challenge (2026-06-02) runs random MLPs at depth 32, then 1024 wide × 16 deep, with no official results at the capture. ## Describing a layer by cumulants (K = 2)

With K = 2 a layer's activations are summarised by a mean μ and covariance Σ over the input distribution for one fixed network. The linear step is exact: μ ↦ Wμ, Σ ↦ WΣWᵀ. The nonlinearity, applied neuron by neuron assuming Gaussian pre-activations, gives a new mean from a 1-D Gaussian integral and a new covariance entry from a 2-D one; for zero-mean ReLU it is the closed-form arc-cosine kernel (Cho and Saul 2009). The only learned parameters stay W, and the nonlinearity adds none. This is the NNGP / deep information propagation recursion (Neal 1996; Poole et al. 2016; Schoenholz et al. 2017; Lee et al. 2018) applied to one fixed network (ARC's "quenched average"). Everything non-Gaussian is thrown away at each layer, and the error grows like (L/n)².

It is not a compression in general: a k-th cumulant tensor has nᵏ entries against n² for a weight matrix, so K ≥ 3 is larger than the weights unless factorised. With isotropic inputs the first layer depends on W only through WWᵀ. For trained networks the right description is probably the few statistics the network relies on; for our deduplication model that would be residuals, counts and group sums plus the maps between them, and predicting its KL to Bayes better than sampling episodes would meet the bar in our setting.

## A network defined by cumulant maps

Kelvin asked whether a network could be defined only by cumulant transformations, with no weights. The assistant's answer (its reasoning, `inferred`):

- **No error inside such a network.** Kelvin's point, accepted: "error" exists only relative to an outside reference; for a network defined by its own maps the question is expressiveness and fit. If the states are to be read as beliefs they must stay realisable as moments (variances non-negative, covariances positive semidefinite, H consistent with H = A·B), which narrows the allowed maps.
- **Pairwise products are native.** E[AB] = μ_A μ_B + Σ_AB, so a layer with bilinear terms computes a product exactly, for any input distribution; XOR of ±1 variables is P(Y = 1) = (1 − E[AB])/2. A product's own variance needs old fourth moments and its covariance with a third variable needs third moments, so those must be filled by an assumption or skipped when nothing downstream needs them. For ±1 variables the Walsh coefficients make variances follow from means.
- **3-way parity is not impossible.** The assistant retracted its "impossible at K = 2" claim after Kelvin's challenge: with point or independent inputs two bilinear layers give abc, and k-way parity needs about log₂k layers. The only hard limit is information the input summary lacks: two input distributions with the same means and covariances can differ in E[ABC], and no architecture can recover it.
- **Unconstrained, it is a known architecture.** A vector plus a pair matrix with bilinear updates is the AlphaFold Evoformer, linear-attention fast weights, bilinear layers (Shazeer 2020; Pearce et al. 2024 read them by eigendecomposition) and polynomial networks. It cannot be weight-free (a generic bilinear map on n + n² numbers has O(n⁶) parameters, so structure such as Σ′ = WΣWᵀ brings weights back), adds no expressive power over MLPs, and with point inputs Σ stays 0 so it reduces to an ordinary MLP. Kelvin: "Alright, let us ignore it then."
- **One live link to the agenda (open).** A persistent pair state makes GLS deduplication cheap, because the weights need the source covariance (w ∝ Σ⁻¹1); a belief-state arm with learned update Σ′ = (Σ⁻¹ + HᵀR⁻¹H)⁻¹ would make deduplication exact by construction, with shared noise in R. Whether it deduplicates in one layer is the comparison to make if depth turns out to be how plain transformers deduplicate (see [[pfn-deduplication-and-wind-tunnel-mechanisms]]). Natural-parameter and moment-propagation networks are related prior work, cited from memory.

## Reference distribution, rare events and anomaly detection

Asked how cumulant propagation relates to inputs from an unknown distribution, the assistant agreed it cannot: for one input you just run the network. ARC still wants it for (a) rare-event estimation under a hand-specified distribution (Wu and Hilton 2024) and (b) mechanistic anomaly detection, where the explanation is built on a trusted reference distribution (known through samples, so empirical moments suffice) and a new input is flagged if the output depends on terms the explanation dropped. Both papers are cited from memory. For us the method transfers only if a question takes the form "behaviour under a distribution we can name"; the in-context case inside one world needs a defined distribution over contexts first.

## What would count as a significant result for a trained PFN

Kelvin asked whether a mechanistic estimate beating sampling on random inputs, without ARC's weight perturbation, would matter. The assistant's answer, which Kelvin accepted ("We need a mechanistic understanding of the errors from Bayes"):

- **Two cheap wins must be ruled out.** (1) A stored answer: for one fixed network E_x[f(x)] is one number, computable once by heavy sampling, so ARC averages over weight noise or seeds; the substitute for us is predicting a whole family of quantities (any world prior or context condition, including unseen ones) or using independently trained seeds. (2) "The network is Bayes": on prior inputs the exact posterior is almost free and accurate, but its error is a fixed δ² (the network's deviation from Bayes) while sampling's σ²/N keeps falling, so Bayes wins only below the compute where σ²/N < δ². It also adds no understanding, since "≈ Bayes" was known from the training objective.
- **The bar** is a mechanistic estimate of the network's deviation from Bayes, across a family of input distributions, with MSE below sampling past the crossover, over several independently trained seeds, with the explanation fixed before held-out conditions. Truly random inputs such as Gaussian tokens are off-distribution and tell us less about aggregation. As far as the assistant knows, nobody has reached ARC's bar on a trained transformer of any size.
- **Known input distribution does not make it easy.** Kelvin expected that with the training distribution fully known every neuron's moments could be known. The assistant: measured is not explained (a lookup table); n^k joint moments are too many and which matter is the explanation; and moments averaged over the training distribution blur the per-episode deviation, with activations over a mixture of worlds multimodal. Proposed instead: condition on the episode's exact Bayes sufficient statistics and write each neuron as those plus a residual, so δ must live in the residuals. Roles are easy to find (probes decode the sufficient statistics) but a 95%-variance description leaves more than δ unexplained, and probes overstate use, hence untrained controls and interventions.
- **Order of work.** Understand the approximate-Bayes circuit before its errors: locate each Bayes intermediate (retrained probes with an untrained control), intervene (swap it with another episode's value), then replace the component with the hypothesised computation and score the fraction of behaviour retained. The first deliverable is, per layer, which intermediate it carries and that fraction, on the deduplication models once they finish ([[pfn-deduplication-and-wind-tunnel-mechanisms]] holds the run).

Why single neurons resist this, and the two cases where they do not, is argued on [[single-neuron-interpretability-tractability]].

## Making cumulant propagation easy: architecture and training

Error has three sources (the assistant's analysis, references from memory): the nonlinearity creating higher cumulants; shared inputs (reconvergent paths, the reason loopy belief propagation is approximate); and compounding over depth, tamed by width as roughly 1/n. Candidate fixes, each aimed at one source:

- residual branches scaled by 1/√L (Hayou and Yang 2023: infinite-width and infinite-depth limits commute), the strongest candidate but a result for random weights at initialisation;
- low-degree polynomial or Hermite-truncated activations, which remove the integral error but not the truncation, since moment order still blows up with depth;
- sparse tree-like connectivity, which needs input dimension fan-in^L;
- normalisation that keeps per-layer gain near 1.

**The tension.** In the Roberts-Yaida picture the L/n corrections are where feature learning lives, and a network exactly described by low-order cumulants sits near the kernel regime that does not learn features. Whether a parametrisation keeps estimation error small after training while still learning features is open (depth-μP is the nearest candidate); no result on estimation error after training was found.

**Training for estimability (open idea).** A literature check found nobody who penalises the gap between propagated cumulants and sampled statistics. Nearest work: verifiability training (Xiao et al. 2019; Lyu 2021; De Palma 2022; Shi 2021; Mao et al. 2024, where interval-bound training raises propagation tightness to about 0.98 against near 0 for ordinary training, at some cost in clean accuracy); moment-propagation networks that train through propagated moments without penalising their error (Wu et al. 2019; analytic covariance 2403.16163; MP-GELU; stable-distribution propagation); and ARC, which treats an estimator-versus-sampling gap as structure to learn, not a penalty. Two warnings: it costs accuracy where tried, and the cheapest way to satisfy the penalty is to become near-linear or kernel-like. Kelvin asked for a full test, and a subagent ran it: a pre-norm transformer at depth 8 (depth 4 as contrast) on a smaller deduplication world, K = 4 with K = 2 alongside, penalty weights λ = 0.3, 3 and 30 against λ = 0, at least 3 seeds, preregistered, GPU spend capped at $15 (about $12.2 spent at the $0.87/h ceiling rate, counting two VMs lost before running anything). Report `estimability-penalty-2026-10-27.md` in the mats outbox, not opened by this wiki and without an adversarial review.

- **Result: the penalty works but costs accuracy.** The error of the propagated estimate (variance-normalised squared error of E[μ], E[log σ], E[NLL] against 2²² Monte Carlo samples) falls 9×/74×/45× at depth 8 for λ = 0.3/3/30 and 30×/191×/83× at depth 4; the K = 2 arm gives 14× (depth 8) and 47× (depth 4). The gap to exact Bayes NLL rises from 0.011 to 0.0136-0.0149 nats (+0.0027 to +0.0041, 9-40 standard errors), about 25-38% of the remaining gap. It passes the preregistered "no degradation" tolerance of 0.005 nats only because that tolerance was generous; the assistant calls it a trade-off.
- **Still loses to sampling.** For E[NLL] the propagator matches at most about 2.7k samples while one propagation costs 38k-94k forward passes, so sampling wins by 15-50×, before training too (initial equivalent about 700 samples at depth 8, 2.2k at depth 4). The ARC bar is not met on this propagator. The assistant's read: the branch has given its answer, nothing here earns more spend.
- **How the penalty is met.** Not by going linear or kernel-like (a random-features baseline is 20× worse on the task, weights move only slightly more, attention entropy barely changes), the warning above notwithstanding. The residual stream becomes nearly Gaussian instead: excess kurtosis falls from about 1.2 to 0.1 and skew from about 0.7 to 0.04, and LayerNorm input norms steady.
- **K = 4 adds nothing** over K = 2 in any arm or at initialisation: the affordable third and fourth cumulants cannot represent LayerNorm's cross-feature non-Gaussianity, the extra terms are satisfied by removing non-Gaussianity rather than predicting it, and full K = 4 would need about 10¹⁰ entries per tensor at this width. Most remaining error is in attention (the attention-weight × value product); trained models also need a positive-semidefinite projection of the propagated covariance.
- **Deviations from the preregistration**, recorded before any penalty result: no duplicate rows (keeps the attention mask fixed), exact per-partition input cumulants instead of a large sample, the penalty applied every 8th step at weight 8λ because a penalty step costs 3-8 s on an L4, and depth 8 split across three VMs.

**Bilinear and polynomial attention (parked).** Kelvin pointed to Logan Riggs' LessWrong post on bilinear MLPs and attention (500M-parameter models on FineWeb). The assistant's reading: the models are not polynomial as trained (RMSNorm before every block, QK-norm, a tanh logit cap), "scale-invariant" fails because the residual stream mixes degrees, and the evidence is thin (one run per arm, softmax-tuned hyperparameters, 10% more parameters in the bilinear attention). A truly polynomial network would make moment propagation exact in closed form but degree still multiplies per block, so truncation remains. Unnormalised bilinear attention would give m identical sources weight m, a clean contrast for whether attention's normalisation helps deduplication. Kelvin: "Let us park this tensor exploration"; the two possible arms (polynomial attention in the estimability test, bilinear attention in a dedup follow-up) are recorded in the assistant's pending-discussion note, not started.
