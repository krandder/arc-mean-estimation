**2.2 (K) The "k\* bits through random gates" argument, first form (30 Oct).** "on any deep enough network, we can show that n effectively collapses to k\* effective dimensions minimum that need to be kept/understood, and that they also interact in a fully-connected way, and pass through random gates. He also said: "I am disappointed by the hardness lane not coming up (or explaining why this argument of mine doesn't work)." **2.3 (K) Two-part refinement: a scrambling middle and a decoding end that is "hard to average" (30-31 Oct).** The assistant objected that a hash's average is a random function's average, which can be computed without sampling (the presumption-of-independence answer). Kelvin replied: "its not *only* a hash. Its a hash, and later a 'final layers' over the result of the hash. You can model it as the output of the hash having a average that is simple, but then you need to basically measure each possible output to see the mean for each output and marginalize over outputs." (10-30 22:41). And on 31 Oct: "We need to compose two different things. We need to understand the 'middle', which is where we show the shuffling picture ... You can easily compute the 'average' of the random parts/bits I guess; but that is not what we want. Because there is the 'final part' which I think behaves in a way that is like, hard to average over ... We can track the initial layers via the momentum strategies, because it starts as gaussian. But for the latest layers, it starts already with the non-gaussian distribution, that carries 'random bits' inside." (10-31 11:41). Status: superseded by 2.5. Note the location claim: early layers are easy (Gaussian), the difficulty is late. The measurements put the closure error early (section 7, tension 1).

How he wants to be read here: "I know this. I'm not stupid. I'm talking about an abstract space here." (10-30 22:41), after the assistant pointed out that the k\* directions rotate with the input.

**2.5 (K) The pseudorandom-code conjecture, latest form (3 Nov 00:24).** Verbatim: "My idea is that the 'scrambling' of bits, if 'fully pseudorandom' in a way, combined with a 'decoding' step at the end/final layers where basically each 'code' before the decoding has a different 'mean', and the way to estimate the mean is basically to sum over p(code) \* E(mean|code), and that it seems difficult to do much better at this than just sampling code and mean|code. Only for extremely small networks you can map the code or the regions and integrate precisely much much better than sampling, like we saw. This is what I have in mind now, but it seems extremely informal." The assistant's formalisation (A?, no recorded objection): E[f] = Σ_c p(c)·m(c), m(c) = E[f | c]; if the input-to-code map is pseudorandom and m(c) varies, no efficient algorithm reaches precision ε much faster than sampling's ~1/ε². The catch it added: if m(c) depended on the code additively, unit by unit, the sum would split into easy pieces. So the hard case needs high-order joint dependence: "the output mean depends on the input law only through high-order correlations (roughly growing with depth) that behave pseudorandomly". Refutation condition: a short "explanation" of the high-order structure that an algorithm can use. Status: live as of 3 Nov, then the thread went quiet. The 9 Nov agenda does not mention it. Formal status in 3.4 and 4.

- Two fits: "the fit of the input to the expert, and also the fit of the expert to predicting that output (that internal mean). These two things need to be weighted together" (16:31).
- Precision fusion: offered a choice between mixture weighting and inverse-variance weighting ("experts as independent noisy measurements of a single truth"), he chose: "I think this is actually what we need. (16:37); "Maybe this way we can even learn to error-correct the experts?" (16:39).
- Joint confidence and targeted sampling: "for a given (already trained) population of experts, we could distinguish some prediction as high-confidence or low-confidence ... we are not really keeping track of the 'final joint confidence' ... this may be important in case we want to do further sampling of whatever parts we're not fully confident in ... ## 3. Formal objects and designs (as stated in the sources)

**3.1 The estimand and the bar.** h_0 = x ~ N(0, I_n), h_l = relu(W_l h_{l−1}), W_l iid N(0, 2/n), no biases. The estimand is the vector E_x h_L (all n outputs). The error is nMSE = |est − E f|² / tr Var f, so N MC samples score 1/N. Cost is counted in forward passes (2n²L flops). ARC's matching-sampling principle: an estimator running in time O((1/ε²)·Time(M)) with error ≤ ε² E Var for all ε, that is "mechanistic" (heuristically, it "avoids any random or pseudorandom sampling"). Phase 1 is 256 x 32 with 64,850 passes; Phase 2 is 1024 x 16. ARC's conjecture for order-K cumulant propagation: MSE ≤ c_K (L/n)^K at runtime c′_K L n^{K+1} (basic), i.e. about n^{K−1} passes. ARC App. D: "This depth scaling is worse than Monte Carlo sampling".

**3.2 Pricing identities (arc-update, FORMAL A1-A6; labels as in the source).** Δ_l = Q_l − Φ_l Q_{l−1} is the defect of a reconstruction Q_l, and g_l the map from layer l to the output.
- A1 (PROVEN): est − Y = Σ_l ⟨g_l, Δ_l⟩.
- A2 (PROVEN, every finite width): for any past-measurable defect Δ at layer l with t = L − l layers to go, E_future |⟨g_l, Δ⟩|² = Σ_{d≥0} b_d^{(n,t)} ‖∫ |h| (h/|h|)^{⊗d} dΔ(h)‖_F², with b_d ≥ 0, Σ_d b_d = 1, b_0 ≥ 1/π and nondecreasing in t. Proof: Mehler's formula plus positive homogeneity. "No forward method can know which directions of its cloud will matter."
- A3: matching moments through order K cancels M_1 and, on a common sphere, M_d for d ≤ K, but not in general the radius feature M_0 or M_d for d > K.
- A4: κ_r(z_i) = ⟨w_i^{⊗r}, T⟩; E_w⟨w^{⊗r}, T − T̂⟩² ≥ r!(2/n)^r ‖T − T̂‖_F²; with flattening rank ≤ k, ‖T − T̂‖² ≥ the tail of T's flattening spectrum beyond k. This prices approximation, not storage.
- A5 (terminal model): E_{W_L}|est − Y|² = Σ_d b_d^{(n,1)} ‖M_d(Q_{L−1} − P_{L−1})‖², with b^{(1)} = (1/π, 1/2, 1/(2π), 0, 1/(24π), 0, 1/(80π), …).
- Infinite-width spectrum: Σ_d d·b_d = 1 exactly, so the mean degree of the b_{≥2} mass is (1 − b_1)/(1 − b_0 − b_1). As t → ∞: 1 − b_0 ~ 9π²/(2t²), b_1 = Θ(t^{−3}), and the mean degree grows like 2t²/(9π²) (PROVEN asymptotics). At t = 31: b_0 = 0.973, b_1 = 0.003, b_{≥2} = 0.024 with mean degree 42.3. Finite width at 256, t = 31: b_0 = 0.948 (measured).

**3.3 The ladder as an error-cost exponent (arc-update 2.5; PROVEN UNDER a uniform lower law).** If e_K ≥ a c_* (L/n)^K and C_K ≥ d_* n^{K−1}, uniformly in K, then e_best(C) ≥ a c_* (L/n)(C/d_*)^{−γ} with γ = log(n/L)/log n. At L = αn, γ → 0. At 256 x 32, γ = 0.375; the measured order 2→3 values are 0.52, 0.39, 0.23, 0.30 at n = 32-256. Slogan: "escape this ladder or lose". There is no positive floor; the conclusion does not need one.

**3.4 The pseudorandom-code conjecture and its three formal versions (wiki).** (1) A restricted lower bound: any method using only degree-≤d statistics of the input or of each layer has error ≥ X. (2) A conditional bound: if the code map is pseudorandom against degree-d tests, a lower bound follows. (3) An unconditional bound, which would be a super-linear time lower bound and is out of reach. The equivalence the assistant stated: "input statistics up to degree d do not pin down the output mean; equivalently, no degree-d polynomials p₋ ≤ f ≤ p₊ have close Gaussian means" (a sandwiching / LP-duality statement).

**3.5 The order-127 witness (co-prover; the wiki had not checked the proof).** Input: 256 Gaussian coordinates; among the first 128, the |x_i| are independent but the product of signs is forced to be +1. Every statistic on ≤ 127 coordinates matches N(0, I). Net: f(x) = ReLU(Π sign(x_1…x_128) · min |x_i|), built with (|a+b| − |a−b|)/2 = sign(ab)·min(|a|,|b|) in a 7-level binary tree, bias-free, width 256. Gap: E f = ½E[min|G_i|] under the Gaussian against E[min|G_i|] under the constrained law, a factor 2. The co-prover calls it "a worst-case counterexample within the architecture, not a lower bound for iid Gaussian weights".

**3.6 Three readings of "scrambled when correctly compressed" (arc-update section 5).**
- Minimal output-determining description: every layer carries exactly the output's bits and the transition can be the identity. Nothing is scrambled and nothing is gained, because computing the code means running the future (PROVEN).
- Layer-local encoder: consecutive codes are related by near-linear threshold maps (R² 0.82-0.99; 96% of the predictable part), MEASURED. Without scrambled positive controls this reads "the tests found low degree". A PROVEN separation: cheap transitions do not imply a cheap law (a weighted threshold is linear-time to evaluate, but its probability counts knapsack solutions).
- Tower property (PROVEN, one line): with decoder G(c) = E[a | c], Σ_c P(c)G(c) = E a at any rate. So the bits needed "at bias level" are zero. The whole cost lies in computing P(c) and E[a | c].
- The frame is scrambled (PROVEN): given the past, the next layer is a fresh Gaussian matrix, and its neurons read the cloud's cumulants through independent rank-one probes.
- The exact forward object for a positively homogeneous net is ν(du) = E[R | U = u] P(du) on the sphere. It propagates linearly by a gain-weighted push-forward, and every moment of ν is read by some deep future (b_d > 0).
- Conclusion as stated: "the layer-to-layer dynamics of compressed codes are not scrambled (as far as the tests can see); the bits are scrambled relative to the coordinates in which the law is computable." The mean Hermite degree of the value function is 21, 7.7, 4.1, 1.9, 1.3, 1.04 at l = 0, 4, 8, 16, 24, 31, and the annealed mean degree of the input map is n/E_W V. "'hash' ... is the wrong picture for these methods; 'random-orientation reads of a high-dimensional, high-degree structure that is made early' is the better one."

**3.7 Cumulant ambiguity (lane report; PROVEN items rest on co-prover rounds 1-2).**
- Claim 0: the output mean is linear in the order-1 moment of the last activation layer, so "cumulants agree at every layer up to the output yet means differ" is impossible as written; "every layer" has to stop at h_{L−1}.
- Oracle: gap(all) ≤ gap(last) ≤ Σ_i |a_i| Γ_d(μ_i), where μ_i is the law of the last pre-activation. Γ_d → 0 for every fixed net. For a Gaussian pre-activation, Γ_d/sd(relu T) = 0.856 (d = 2, 3), 0.362 (d = 4, 5), ≈ 1.686/d for large d. No general lower bound gap(all) ≥ c·Γ_d.
- Weight-averaged kurtosis of the last pre-activation: 3(1 + 2/n)(1 + 5/n)^{L−1}. For a symmetric law, Γ_4 = (√v/2)(1 − kurtosis^{−1/2}).
- Worst-case closure: gap(all) ≤ gap(input) ≤ prop. The first step is exact for the Bayes-optimal rule.
- Typical-case CONJECTURE: "no order-d local rule beats c_K(l/n)/n by more than a constant; then every such hierarchy's precision exponent → 0 along L = αn." Measured: the per-step error is about c_K(l/n)/n, and the sum over L = n/8 steps is width-independent. The recursion is 1.0-2.2x the per-step sum at L = n/8 (no amplification) and 5-300x at L/n ≥ 1/4.

**3.8 Sampling-side theory.**
- Point-rule pricing (randomness T1-T2, PROVEN): ensemble risk = Σ_{d≥1} a_d B_d(Q), with B_d(Q) = Σ_ij w_i w_j P_d(u_i·u_j). Unions of independently rotated copies of a fixed design have exponent exactly 1.
- Black-box exponent (PROVEN, round 2): the best positive weight-independent rule has exponent in [1 + 2/(n−1), 1 + 3/(n−1)], which is 1.008-1.012 at n = 256.
- Finite-width variance source (PROVEN, astra): E V → S(0) + ½ = 11.15 along L = αn with L/√n → ∞; design gains converge to simplex 2.09, cross-polytope 2.36, Kerdock 3.15.
- Kink tail (PROVEN): V·NS(δ) = Dδ − (2J_kink/(3√π))δ^{3/2} + o(δ^{3/2}); the Hermite tail beyond t is ~ (J_kink/(3π)) t^{−3/2}.
- Preint F7 (PROVEN, unreviewed at synthesis time): E_W nMSE ≥ (c/2 − o(1))/N for point rules, under the even-energy hypothesis H_even. Proxy-free bounds: ≥ 1/(4π²N) for N ≤ n/2 points and ≥ 1/(8π²N) for N ≤ n/4 lines.
- Under a Gaussian surrogate prior, adaptive and nonlinear algorithms do not beat the best nonadaptive linear rule (Traub-Wasilkowski-Woźniakowski; re-proved). The real prior has excess kurtosis 7-9 per output unit.
- White-box surrogate route (PROVEN UNDER its hypothesis): if surrogates cost q^a to build and q^γ to evaluate, have exact means and leave residual variance C q^{−b} uniformly in width, the MSE is T^{−1−(b−γ)/a}. This is "the one route I can state to an exponent above 1 that holds as width grows". Squared-hinge controls cannot touch even degrees ≥ 4.
- Hybrid criterion: an exact-mean control with residual ∝ cost^{−δ} gives MSE ~ C^{−(1+δ)}. Measured δ ≤ 0.25 at n = 64 and ≤ 0.09 at n = 128.

**3.9 Region theory (adaptive regions).**
- Neyman bookkeeping: MSE (Σ p_k σ_k)²/N. If (Σ p_k σ_k)²/Var f ~ K^{−β} and K ~ N^a, the MSE falls like N^{−(1+aβ)}.
- Theorem 1 (PROVEN): a Haar-rotated fixed K-cell partition captures at most Σ_d min(1, (K − 1)/N_d) E_d of the between-cell variance.
- Gate-cell bound for these nets (PROVEN): β_asymptotic ≤ 8/n.
- Facet formula: E[x 1_C] = (2π)^{−1/2} Σ_j g_j P_{n−1}(F_j); on a cell, E[f 1_C] = A_C p_C c_C.
- Settling: a cap of angular radius δ meets about 0.8 n^{3/2} L δ gate boundaries (HEURISTIC).
- Exact polyhedral integration of one fixed net costs polylog(1/ε) (PROVEN, astronomical constants).
- Chebyshev gap of an unpinned unit: −lo·u/(2(u − lo)).

**3.10 Kelvin's linear-experts design as specified (adaptive-regions section 6; his decisions relayed).** Variant (a): zones and landing shares are learned from samples of the same net. Headline: leftover Var(f − g)/Var f against the number of input zones. Each expert has a bump (where it applies), a linear map (what it predicts) and per-output error variances (how much to trust it). Fusion is by inverse variance, with calibration scored on the fused result.

## 4. What was tested and what it showed (brief; flaws noted)

- **ARC's own code at 256 x 32 and along L = n/8 (arc-update; registered).** Question: how large is the gap to sampling, and how does it scale? Result: order 2 wins about 2x against RQMC near 450 passes on 4 of 5 nets; order 3 loses 5-30x; at the Phase-1 budget RQMC is 7-40x better. The per-order error ratio is ~4-8 at a cost of ~n per order, so γ is 0.23-0.52. 44-64% of order 2's error is made by layer 8, by accumulation. Flaws: widths only up to 256; exponents are slopes over a factor of 4-8; the γ argument assumes ARC's law is a uniform lower bound.
- **Scale mode (forum 18298).** Question: does separating a collective amplitude mode escape the ladder? Result: the best order-2-cost deductive estimator measured (2.5x RQMC at 688 passes at 256 x 32); neither escape criterion was met; the two-level exponent equals ARC's at 256 x 32. At Phase 2 (1024 x 16) deduction wins beyond the budget, with exponent 0.83. Flaw: higher levels were not run on these nets.
- **Radius-corrected closure.** Error ≈ L/n² (a post hoc observation). Run as a finite-sample recursion it costs ≥ 2^19 passes per layer; a deductive version would have to cost < ~5 order-2 steps per layer. None was built.
- **Moment matching (input-only).** Question: how much does matching the input's degree-≤d moments pin E f? Result: the gap stays ≥ 0.21 sd and falls roughly like 1/d. Flaws: n ≤ 8; the highest degrees are lower bounds; the parity control did not behave as expected and is unexplained. It rules out only input-only methods.
- **Cumulant ambiguity.** Oracle form false; worst case true but 14-30x larger than ARC's real error; typical form open (3.7).
- **Gate-pattern chains (Kelvin-approved, depth 4, widths 4-8).** Patterns are few per layer, transitions sparse and low rank (~3n), and each gate depends on 2.5-6 parent bits. But every pattern-indexed summary (means, full history, a Gaussian per pattern, mixtures, a Gaussian per path with 20x more states than cells) stays 100x or more worse than MC. Reason (HEURISTIC): the state inside a cell is a truncated Gaussian cone, and the next gate depends on position inside it. The plateau in cell counts at depth was the net dying.
- **Exact cells and the cone tree.** At 4 x 4, exact cells reach the truth's noise floor, 6-8 orders below MC. Cells grow 3-4x per unit width; by 12 x 4 a million paths no longer list them. Merging gives a factor of 2-3 that does not improve with width.
- **Certified-linear probe.** Certification (IBP/CROWN, boxes, slices, pins) holds ≥ 50% coverage only at the last hidden layer, at every width.
- **Learned experts (A4, A5).** They do not beat averaging their own training samples, even at 4 x 4. Smooth bumps blur the cone boundaries, and the resulting bias is 1e-7 to 1e-3. Flaws: no run passed the doubling check; truths were noisy; budgets were unmatched; there was hold-out leakage in A5.
- **Randomness / structured sampling.** Kerdock 5-design 2.9x over MC, scrambled Sobol 2.1x; fitted exponents 1.0-1.13; the estimated black-box ceiling at ARC's budget is ~10-19x (a plug-in, not certified). White-box frames give at most a few tens of percent.
- **Pre-integration.** It raises the exponent at n = 4-8 but loses at equal cost everywhere: by 21-1,410x along the ladder. A line is worth about one point and costs ~nL/2 passes.
- **Estimability penalty (trained PFN, earlier).** Propagation error falls 9-191x, at a cost in accuracy, by making the residual stream Gaussian; it still loses to sampling by 15-50x. This led to Kelvin's "symbolic activations" note.

## 5. Kelvin's tastes and standards

- **Progress means a major advance or a fundamental blocker.** "we're looking for a major advance" (relayed). For ARC that means major mechinterp progress on the random net, or a major argument that ARC should update. A restricted negative "for the methods tested" did not meet his bar.
- **He cares about asymptotic exponents, not constants.** Hence his reaction to designs that only move constants: "That seems useless, unfortunately."
- **He is willing to spend compute to get a better asymptote:** "overkill" now, better scaling later, and advice compute not counted.
- **He thinks in typical cases, not adversarial ones**, and asks for positive and negative controls.

- **He prefers information-theoretic grounding** (noise, bits) when it is available, but drops it when it does not price the problem (2.13).

- **Tone:** humble toward ARC; no "Claude-like" phrasing; remove content that does not promise gains.
- **He wants the epistemics of a model:** which predictions it is confident in and which it cannot make, with sampling targeted at the uncertain parts.

## 7. Tensions

1. **Where the difficulty sits.** Kelvin's picture (31 Oct): early layers are trackable by moments because they start Gaussian, and the hard part is a late "decoding" over a non-Gaussian law carrying "random bits". The measurements say the reverse for the tested closures: 44-64% of order 2's error is made by layer 8. Early non-Gaussian structure is high-dimensional (third-cumulant participation ratio ~115 at l = 1, collapsing to 1-4 after l ≈ 12). The value function is read at high degree near the input (Hermite degree 21) and at low degree near the output (≤ 4). His conjecture's "decoding step at the end" corresponds to the low-degree, easy end. What makes the problem hard is that no layer offers both a computable law and a low read-out degree.
2. **Pseudorandom codes against a stable output.** The gate pattern is maximally noise-sensitive (some gate flips with probability 0.96 at δ = 1e-6). The output is Lipschitz, decorrelates linearly with slope n/E V ≈ 19, and has a ~22% linear part. A hash argument has to cross from scrambled gates to a smooth output. The randomness lane names this as "the gap it has to close".
3. **The moment-matching evidence mostly measures one kink, not scrambling.** A single 1-D ReLU needs degree 16 to reach 0.1 sd, and the cumulant lane proves Γ_d ≈ 1.69/d for one ReLU of a Gaussian. At n = 4 the measured decay exponent is 0.9-1.1 at every depth: depth raises the constant, not the rate. That is the single-kink rate. So the experiment's 1/d law is what one ReLU already gives, and it is weak evidence for the "high-order pseudorandom correlations" form of his conjecture. This is my reading from the reported numbers; the lanes did not draw it. Separately, the wiki's heuristic Hermite tail "k^(−2/3)" (data source not recorded) conflicts with the randomness lane's PROVEN asymptotic kink tail ~ t^{−3/2} at fixed net. The two may describe different regimes, pre-asymptotic and asymptotic, but no source reconciles them.
4. **Unlimited advice against the "small W" hypothesis.** Kelvin allowed advice larger than the network and did not count its compute (2.19, 2.11). But a future-aware state can be the answer itself (the quantised mean), and he conceded the memorisation loophole. ARC handles this by making estimation error an average over weight noise. The current agenda's crucial hypothesis, that the goal space W needs far fewer parameters than the search policy, is the opposite stance: the target must be small and the heuristic may be large. In the ARC thread he never stated what restriction on the advice keeps the problem non-trivial (held-out distributions? noise over weights? advice size?).
5. **Noise or no noise.** "I dont want to deal with noiseless, this is a separate problem that has no information theory understanding" (10-29 00:11), then "we can ignore the noise of the layers and work on the noiseless networks that ARC uses, if that is easier" (11-02 15:23). The information-theoretic grounding he valued was given up for the ARC lanes. The tower property then showed that bits do not price estimation anyway.
6. **"Hopeless" alongside "have some faith".** The two coexist as a deliberate stance. In practice the positive attempts were run on 4 x 4 to 8 x 4 nets, where exact cells already win, and then judged by whether they scale.
7. **The MCTS analogy against the structure of the problem.** MCTS prunes because its target is a max. Mean estimation is a sum, so nothing can be dropped without certified bounds, and certification failed one layer back. His current agenda needs both: beliefs (sums or expectations) and plans (max). The same search machinery behaves differently on the two (section 8, gem 1).
8. **Locally consistent closures against global accuracy.** The set of all layer laws consistent with true order-d statistics at each step spans ~1 sd of output mean (the worst case). Yet ARC's particular closure lands at 0.01-0.03 sd. So local consistency alone leaves T badly undetermined, and a selection rule does the work. The current agenda treats consistency as the loss that defines T; this result says consistency underdetermines T unless it is paired with a canonical choice (section 9).

## 8. Dropped or underexplored gems for the current agenda

1. **"A sum, not a max": beliefs cannot be searched like plans without bounds.** The assistant's objection to Kelvin's MCTS analogy (2 Nov 15:02) is a precise answer to one of his open items, "whether beliefs and plans can be searched for 'in almost the same way'". For a plan, the value is a max, and a branch can be abandoned once it is clearly worse. For a belief that is an expectation, every region contributes in proportion to its mass, and a branch can be abandoned only when a certified bound times its mass fits the error budget (settle-ability). Policy independence is easy for sums (any refinement order gives the same limit) and pruning is hard. For max it is the reverse: pruning is natural, but the result can depend on the policy at finite compute. The ARC lanes measured this concretely: certification fails one layer back. The current agenda's encoding of utility as a prior via a Faith edge turns planning into belief (a sum-like object). It therefore inherits the sum's inability to prune unless the Faith edge's sharpness restores max-like behaviour. This trade-off has not been examined.

2. **Kelvin's linear experts with validity zones, confidences and fusion are a PDG.** Each expert is a conditional belief with a validity zone (where it applies) and a per-output confidence (a β), fused by inverse variance. He chose that over mixture weighting ("experts as independent noisy measurements of a single truth"). His double-counting question (two near-identical experts each claiming variance 1 fuse to 0.5) is the deduplication problem of his aggregation thread, and PDG/LIR has the same issue when several edges carry the same information. The assistant's fix, calibrating the fused output and not each expert, is a concrete rule that could carry over to how LIR edge confidences are learned. The separate point he queried (11-02 17:23), that experts covering different sub-populations must add by mass (mixture) while experts about the same point must multiply (product), is the sum/product distinction of gem 1 again. A PDG fuses as a product; an expectation over a population is a mixture.

3. **"Symbolic activations" (27 Oct).** Rather than penalising non-Gaussianity, make activations follow known rules, by an EM analogy, so that estimation is tractable by construction. For an explicit mesa-optimizer, which is built rather than understood, this is the design principle: choose representations whose push-forward is computable, so that T is computable in the limit. The estimability test showed that the cheap way to satisfy a propagation loss is to Gaussianise, at a cost in accuracy. A symbolic or discrete-latent target space W is the alternative he named and never developed. His later question about an architecture "where each output of each layer is strictly 'binary' and softmaxed" while staying critical (11-02 12:24) points the same way.

4. **The control-variate identity as a template for a policy-independent T.** E f = E[g] + E[f − g] is unbiased for every surrogate g; g changes only variance. This is "the heuristic changes speed, never the target" in its simplest exact form, with a natural consistency loss, Var(f − g). Kelvin said he did not understand it (28 Oct). The lanes then used it only as a scoring device for hybrids on random nets (δ shrinks with width). It deserves a second look as the formal skeleton of amplify/distill: the distilled network is g, the amplified estimate is the unbiased hybrid, and the distillation loss is the residual variance.

5. **Exact cells win outright where regions are few.** At 4 x 4, exact integration over ~100-200 cells is 6-8 orders of magnitude below MC. Everything failed where the count of relevant regions explodes, which happens 3-4x per unit width for random nets. This is the empirical shadow of his "W is small" hypothesis: deduction is unbeatable when the target-relevant partition is small. Random nets are a case with no small partition, and nothing in the corpus measures region counts relevant to a target in trained nets.

6. **The pricing identity as a query-weighted inconsistency measure.** A2 says a downstream computation reads a belief's defect only through nonnegative weights b_d on radially normalised moments, determined by the future, not by the belief. That is a principled "how much does this edge's error matter for this query" weighting. Kelvin dreamed of exactly this on 28 Oct: "One can dream of asking questions and then our divergence for the question automatically finds the structure that explains things." In LIR terms, the inconsistency worth resolving should be measured in the query's kernel norm, not a uniform KL.

7. **The radius-weighted direction measure ν.** For positively homogeneous computations the exact forward object is not the activation law but ν(du) = E[R | U = u] P(du), which propagates linearly. It is a reminder that the right state of an amplification procedure may be a query-specific transform of the belief rather than the belief itself. This is a narrow technical gem.

## 9. Leverage points

1. **ARC's heuristic-estimator formalism already contains his martingale desideratum.** Established: ARC has work on heuristic estimators with an "iterated expectations" coherence requirement. The Robinson wiki page lists "the heuristic-explanations, surprise-accounting and iterated-expectations coherence work" as ARC-general. FROM MEMORY, unverified: Christiano, Neyman and collaborators (around 2024, "Towards a law of iterated expectations for heuristic estimators") ask that an estimator's estimate of its own future estimate, after more arguments, equal its current estimate. This is the same as Kelvin's "amplified belief approximately a martingale over further computation". I also recall impossibility or tension results between this property, an error-orthogonality property and accuracy. If so, they bear directly on whether his T can be both a martingale and accurate. Action: read those papers before formalising T. Conjecture: his "policy independence" corresponds to their requirement that the estimate not depend on the order in which arguments arrive.

2. **Probabilistic numerics gives a ready-made T with martingale and policy independence, and says when search helps.** Established in the sources: under a Gaussian prior on the integrand, optimal algorithms are affine and adaptation does not help (Traub-Wasilkowski-Woźniakowski, cited in preint and re-proved there). FROM MEMORY: Bayesian quadrature / Diaconis's "Bayesian numerical analysis" defines the estimate as the posterior mean of the integral given the evaluations made. That posterior mean is a martingale over computation for any adaptive policy, and it converges to the true value. So T = lim posterior mean is policy-independent, and the policy affects only speed, which is Kelvin's ingredient (3). The sharp lesson: under Gaussian priors a clever search policy buys nothing, because adaptation is useless. The value of the "alien" search policy therefore comes entirely from non-Gaussian structure (here, excess kurtosis 7-9 per output unit). This gives a testable criterion for when the policy network matters, and a precise place where priors enter T. Conjecture: an LIR-style amplification can be cast as approximate Bayesian computation over the uncomputed parts of M, with the prior playing the role of ARC's presumption of independence.

3. **Consistency underdetermines T; a selection rule must be part of the definition.** Established (cumulant ambiguity): the closures consistent with the true order-d statistics at each step span ~1 sd of output mean at n = 4, while ARC's max-entropy or Edgeworth-type closure errs by 0.01-0.03 sd. Max-entropy at order 4 is 2-3x worse than Edgeworth, so the choice of rule matters by constant factors. For the current agenda: making the amplified belief consistent does not by itself fix T for an uncertain model. A canonical choice is needed (max-entropy, minimum PDG inconsistency with fixed β, or a prior). Richardson's PDG semantics already pairs inconsistency with an entropy term, which is a selection rule of this kind. Conjecture: the γ-weighted PDG semantics with γ > 0 is the analog of ARC's closure, and its typical-case accuracy, not its consistency, is what should be studied.

4. **The typical-case closure lower bound is a clean, citeable open theorem.** Conjecture (cumulant lane): no local order-d closure rule beats per-step error c_K(l/n)/n by more than a constant. If true, every moment hierarchy's precision exponent tends to 0 along L = αn. This is the restricted negative "in ARC's own terms" that would meet his bar for a major argument. FROM MEMORY, unverified: the low-degree-polynomial and statistical-query frameworks (Hopkins; Kunisky-Wein-Bandeira) are the standard tools for "typical-case hardness for local or low-degree procedures". The co-prover's "Gaussian-promise lower bound (S5)" is named as the open route. This is also the honest answer to his request for related hard problems.

5. **Reduction candidates for "which hard problems relate" (his deferred question).** All FROM MEMORY, unverified, and all conjectural as reductions:
   - CAPP (the circuit acceptance probability problem), where deterministic additive approximation is derandomisation. The randomness lane already showed that derandomisation is free on random weights, so this is the wrong target.
   - Continuous LWE (Bruna-Regev-Song-Tang) and the hardness of learning periodic or cosine single neurons (Song-Zadik-Bruna), which give planted random-looking structure in Gaussian-input networks.
   - Known quadratic quantum speedups for mean estimation (amplitude estimation), which show 1/ε² is not information-theoretic for white-box functions.
   A planted variant, "random net plus a hidden low-degree-invisible shift such as the order-127 parity", is where a reduction would start. ARC's random-weight setting has no planted structure, which is why the sources call target 2 out of reach.

6. **Surprise accounting as the measure of "W is small".** Established (arc-matching-sampling-bar): ARC's full-understanding bar counts total surprise, the bits of facts about the weights plus the leftover surprise, against the bits of optimisation that selected the model. For a random net the selection is zero bits, so a full explanation must be almost free, and the lanes show that fails at depth. For trained nets this is the natural quantitative form of the hypothesis that W needs far fewer parameters than the search-policy network: the target-relevant description should cost about as many bits as the selection pressure that created it. Conjecture: random nets are the worst testbed for his hypothesis and trained (selected) nets the right one.

7. **Exact polyhedral integration has a polylog(1/ε) rate at fixed width.** Established (PROVEN, arc-update): for one fixed net a deterministic method eventually beats sampling as ε → 0, with astronomical constants. So "better asymptote as compute → ∞" (his ask) is true at fixed width and fails only uniformly in width. This exactly mirrors MCTS: exact in the limit, with the question being the rate at which the heuristic closes the gap. If the current agenda's T is defined as an infinite-compute limit, ARC's case shows the limit can be clean while every width-uniform rate is bad. Policy independence of T says nothing about whether any policy is fast.
