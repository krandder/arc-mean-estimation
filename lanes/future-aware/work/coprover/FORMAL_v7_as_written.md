## 1. Classes
Definition F-1 (future interface). A layer-wise deduction-projection estimator has future interface (S_l) if its
layer-l reconstruction Q_l is measurable w.r.t. G_l = sigma(W_1..W_l, S_l), with S_l a sigma-algebra generated by
quantities computed from the future F_l = (W_{l+1}..W_L). Forward-only: S_l trivial. Fully future-aware: S_l = sigma(F_l).
Examples: (i) k-sketch: S_l = sigma(W_{l+1} V_l, W_{l+2}, .., W_L), V_l past-measurable n x k orthonormal;
(ii) relevance-subspace closures: S_l = sigma(U_l), U_l = top-k eigenvectors of a cloud-averaged backward statistic of
g_l (active subspace E[J^T J], whitened or not; frozen- or probabilistic-gate Jacobian products);
(iii) future-weighted truncation: S_l = sigma(U_l) and the kept cumulant components are those in U_l (or weighted by
the realised future's reading).

## 2. Pricing given the interface
F-2 (conditional pricing; PROVEN, one line). If Delta is G-measurable then
  E[ |<g_l, Delta>|^2 | G ] = iint K_G(h, h') dDelta(h) dDelta(h'),  K_G(h, h') = E[ g_l(h) . g_l(h') | G ].
Write g_l = m_G + r_G with m_G = E[g_l | G]: K_G = m_G(h).m_G(h') + C_G(h, h'), both positive semi-definite kernels.
The first part is the PREDICTABLE reading (a method in the class could cancel it if it can compute int m_G dDelta);
the second, the residual reading, is a lower bound on the price of every G-measurable defect with the same features:
  E[|<g_l, Delta>|^2 | G] >= iint C_G dDelta dDelta.
F-3 (ARC's L^2 sketching is the annealed case; PROVEN identification). ARC's "mechanistic L^2 sketching" keeps the top
eigenfunctions of the tail kernel E_f[f(x) f(x')] built from the tail's LAW. In the MLP setting that kernel is
Theorem A's: K(h, h') = |h||h'| kappa_t^{(n)}(u . u'), u = h/|h|, kappa^{(n)}_t(s) = sum_d b_d s^d. Expanding s^d in
zonal harmonics of S^{n-1}, K = |h||h'| sum_j beta_j Z_j(u . u') with beta_j = sum_{d >= j, d = j mod 2} b_d c_{d,j}
>= 0: the eigenfunctions are |h| Y_{j,i}(u) (radius times spherical harmonics of degree j, multiplicity ~ n^j/j!) with
eigenvalue proportional to beta_j. ARC's own rule, applied with the tail law, therefore keeps first the radius-weighted
mass int |h| dQ (j = 0), then the n radius-weighted first harmonics, then degree 2, ...: a radially normalised moment
hierarchy. The radius-corrected closure of arc-update (radius law exact) is the first step of this prescription; the
worst-case error after keeping degrees <= j is set by the largest unused beta, i.e. by the slowly decaying high-degree
tail of b_d (section 2.3 of arc-update: mean degree 42 at t = 31, infinite width). (HEURISTIC: the ordering beta_j ~
b_j to leading order in 1/n.)
F-4 (the realised kernel has rank <= n; PROVEN). For S = sigma(F_l), K(h, h') = sum_{i<=n} g_{l,i}(h) g_{l,i}(h'):
rank <= n, and the optimal sketch is the n numbers int g_{l,i} dQ, i.e. the answer. Realised sketching is trivial in
information and is the whole problem in computation [prior: arc-update F2, bit-accounting F1].

## 3. The k-sketch theorem (PROVEN)
Let V be past-measurable n x k with orthonormal columns, P = I - V V^T, B an orthonormal basis of range(P), and
G = sigma(past, W_{l+1} V, W_{l+2}, .., W_L). For h in R^n write a = V^T h, rho = |P h|, v = P h / rho.
Then given G the matrix M = W_{l+1} B is iid N(0, 2/n) (n x (n - k)) and independent of G (Gaussian rotation
invariance: W_{l+1} [V, B] has iid entries). For fixed (a, rho) define G_{a,rho}(xi) = g_{l+1}(relu(W_{l+1} V a +
rho sqrt(2/n) xi)) for xi in R^n; it is G-measurable as a function. For two points h, h', (sqrt(n/2) M B^T v,
sqrt(n/2) M B^T v') are standard Gaussian vectors in R^n with coordinatewise correlation s = v . v'. Mehler's
formula in L^2(gamma_n) with probabilists' Hermite coefficients G^_alpha(a, rho) in R^n gives
  K_G(h, h') = sum_{alpha in N^n} alpha! s^{|alpha|} G^_alpha(a, rho) . G^_alpha(a', rho'),
and therefore, for any G-measurable Delta with int (|h| + |g|) d|Delta| finite,
  E[ |<g_l, Delta>|^2 | G ] = sum_alpha alpha! sum_{i <= n} || int G^_{alpha,i}(a(h), rho(h)) v(h)^{tensor |alpha|}
                                                                   dDelta(h) ||_F^2.
The alpha = 0 term is |int m_G dDelta|^2 with m_G(h) = E_xi G_{a,rho}(xi) (predictable reading, a function of k + 1
variables). The terms |alpha| >= 1 are the residual reading: angular moments of the COMPLEMENT direction v, weighted by
G-measurable functions of the sketched coordinates and the complement radius, read in a rotation-invariant way in the
complement. For k = 0, G^_alpha(rho) = rho G^_alpha(1) (positive homogeneity) and averaging over the later layers gives
Theorem A (b_d = E sum_{|alpha| = d} alpha! |G^_alpha(1)|^2). Proof: as A2, with the fresh block M in place of the whole
first layer; absolute convergence as in A2. QED.
Consequence (PROVEN): a k-sketch method can cancel at most the predictable term; every complement-angular feature of
its defect is priced as in A2 restricted to the (n - k)-dim complement. Defect structure in V's span is (at best) made
free; nothing else changes.

## 4. Future-chosen subspaces (HEURISTIC model, testable)
When U_l is chosen from the future (example (ii)), Theorem 1 of the relevance lane says the future's action on e-perp
is rotation-invariant given the past, so U_l is, apart from the radial direction e and the cloud's own anisotropy, a
uniformly oriented subspace relative to every past-measurable structure, the defect's included.
H-overlap (HEURISTIC): model the realised price of a past-measurable defect as |sum_j lambda_j^{1/2} phi_j|^2 with
(lambda_j) the future's reading spectrum on the cloud (relevance eigenvalues of the part of g_l that the defect
excites, i.e. the non-polynomial part beyond the moments the closure matches) and phi_j the defect's coordinates in the
future's eigenbasis, exchangeable by rotation invariance. Then a projection exact on the top-k future directions
removes in expectation the share s_k = sum_{j <= k} lambda_j / sum_j lambda_j of the price, while a past-measurable
choice (cloud PCA, random) removes ~ the defect's own energy fraction in its k-dim span times the average reading.
So the improvement factor of a future-aware closure over its forward version is ~ 1/(1 - s_k) per cut, where s_k is the
top-k share of the future's NONLINEAR reading (degree >= 3 relative to the Gaussian cloud for mean-and-covariance
matching closures; the radius coupling for others). [prior: relevance] nonlinear-active-subspace counts N_>=2(0.1) = 56,
24, 12, 8 at l = 8, 16, 24, 31 (the nonlinear residual below 10% of V); top-16 linear-plus-nonlinear shares 0.25 (input),
0.58 (l = 4), 0.76 (l = 8), 0.90 (l = 16) under the Gaussian replacement. The closures' errors are made early (P6 of
arc-update: ~60% by layer 8), where s_k is smallest.
CONJECTURE F-W (width): at L = alpha n and fixed l/L, the effective dimension of the future's nonlinear reading grows
~ n (the relevance lane's counts and free-product rule scale with n; the input map's mean degree is n / E_W V
[prior: input-bits, PROVEN annealed]), so for fixed k the share s_k falls with n and a fixed-k future-aware closure's
advantage over its forward version does not grow (it falls) along L = n/8; keeping a fixed share needs k ~ c n.

## 5. Why there is no unconditional extension (PROVEN reduction)
Any algorithm A that outputs an estimate from W in time T is a fully future-aware layer-wise estimator of the same cost
(S_0 = the output of A, carried unchanged; Q_l any reconstruction with int g_l dQ_l = A(W), e.g. a point mass when the
estimate is in the positive orthant's image, or a signed combination otherwise). A lower bound for all future-aware
layer-wise estimators of cost T is a lower bound for all algorithms of cost T on this explicit distributional problem:
a super-linear time lower bound, for which no technique exists [prior: unrestricted 3.3; arc-update section 8]. So a
theorem must restrict the interface (F-1) or the representation. The k-sketch theorem (section 3) is the restriction
that is exactly priceable; relevance-subspace closures are priceable only under H-overlap.

## 6. Costs (accounting used in the experiments)
- Forward pass: 2 n^2 L flops. VJP through t layers: forward plus backward, ~3 x 2 n^2 t flops; one backpropagation
  from the output gives the VJP at every layer at once (~3 passes per sample for all layers).
- Matrix-level backward sweep (frozen or Gaussian-averaged gates, J_l = W_{l+1}^T D_{l+1} J_{l+1}): 2 n^3 flops per
  layer, ~ n passes for all layers; top-k subspaces by k-vector iteration instead: ~ k passes per iteration.
- Exact Gaussian closure: 1184 passes at 256 x 32 [prior: arc-update]; ARC k2 393, k2a 452; radial closure
  ~1.5 J n passes (J quadrature nodes, unknown) [prior].
- A k-dim exact marginal is not deductively affordable for large k (a k-dim integral); deductive members of family
  (a): Gaussian mixtures split along the k future directions (M components, M exact Gaussian steps per layer), or
  cumulants up to order K restricted to the k-dim span (k^K entries per layer plus the pulled-back contractions).
- Hybrid (control variate c with closure mean, RQMC on the residual f - c): error^2 = bias^2 + Var(f - c)/(N G_res),
  bias = E_Q[c] - E_P[c], cost = C_closure + C_backward + N (1 + a_c). It beats a sampler with gain G at total cost C
  iff bias^2 + eta V/(N G_res) < V/(G C), eta = Var(f - c)/V. With bias = 0 (controls in h_1, whose law is explicit)
  this is astra's criterion eta < (G_res/G)/(1 + a_c) (1 - C_setup/C) [prior: arc-update E5].

## 7. Predictions to be registered (directions; numbers after dev calibration)
(i) Single cut: exact-on-k future-relevant directions beats exact-on-k cloud-PCA and random directions, more so at
later cuts; the improvement over the radial oracle is a constant factor (< 3 at l <= 8 for k = 16).
(ii) Full recursion: the future-aware closure's error falls with k slowly early in the net; its gain over MC at fixed k
does not grow with n (F-W).
(iii) Hybrid: controls with exact means (h_1) give eta >= 0.5; controls at deeper layers carry the closure's bias,
which caps the precision near the closure's own error; no hybrid beats RQMC by more than ~2-3x at equal cost.

## E3. The future-weighted ladder (PROVEN arithmetic under stated laws; the criterion is new)
Consider an order-K cumulant hierarchy whose tensors of order 3..K are restricted at every layer to k*-dim
subspaces. Suppose (H-q) its error obeys e_K >= a q^{-K} with a per-order ratio q > 1 (ARC's law: q = n/L), and
(H-k) its cost obeys C_K >= c (k*)^{K-1} passes (each further order multiplies the cost by at least the retained
dimension, the analogue of ARC's factor n for dense tensors), uniformly in K over the orders used. Then at budget C
the affordable orders satisfy K - 1 <= log(C/c)/log k*, and
   e_best(C) >= a q^{-1} (C/c)^{-gamma*},   gamma* = log q / log k*.
Proof: as arc-update's 2.5 with n replaced by k*. QED. Consequences: (i) the dense hierarchy has gamma = log(n/L)/log n;
truncation improves the exponent to log q/log k*; (ii) the hierarchy can match sampling's rate (gamma* >= 1) only if
k* <= q, i.e. the retained dimension must not exceed the per-order error ratio (at ARC's instance q <= n/L = 8, and the
measured q is 4-8 (orders 2 -> 3) and 2-4 (3 -> 4) [prior: arc-update]); (iii) if k* must grow like c n (as for the
exact-marginal family, E2) while q stays bounded at L = alpha n, gamma* -> 0. The decisive measurements are q per order
under truncation and the k* each order needs (registered for order 3 as P6; order 4 is exploratory).
(H-k) is a model of a DEDICATED implementation; the implementation measured here projects ARC's own factorised k3 and
costs at least k3f.

## E4. Why a fixed k loses its effect (HEURISTIC; co-prover round 2 Q2 asks for a proof)
In the mean-field independent-gate model the future's E[J^T J] obeys R_l = W^T [P R_{l+1} P + diag(p(1-p) diag
R_{l+1})] W. The first term propagates the linear relevance, whose participation ratio is ~ n/(2t + 1) [prior:
relevance, gram-chain]: bounded at t = beta n. The second term injects at every layer a diagonal (gate-variance) piece of
weight ~ (1/4) tr R_{l+1} / n per coordinate (p ~ 1/2), which W^T (.) W maps to a nearly isotropic matrix. So the
nonlinear part (R minus the linear part) accumulates a near-isotropic component: its stable rank is Theta(n). A
mean-and-covariance-matching closure's defect is read only by the nonlinear part, so the share s_k of its reading
captured by k directions behaves like k / (c n): fixed k loses its effect as n grows, k = c n keeps it (E2).

## E5. Why the exact-marginal family cannot be cheap deterministically (HEURISTIC, with a PROVEN worst-case core)
Bakhvalov (PROVEN, classical): for Lipschitz integrands on [0,1]^k every deterministic rule with M nodes has worst-case
error >= c_k M^{-1/k}; randomised rules reach M^{-1/2}. A deterministic representation of a k-dim law that serves all
Lipschitz (or piecewise-linear) readings therefore needs M >= eps^{-k} atoms for error eps, against eps^{-2} samples.
With k = c n this is the original curse. What is NOT implied: the future's reading is one specific function and the
law one specific law; structure (low-order cumulants, mixtures) can beat the worst case. The measured structure says
the third cumulant in k = n/8-n/4 future directions is enough for ARC's order-3 accuracy (E2), i.e. low-order
structure is cheap; the exact-marginal gains (18x at k = n/4 at 256) are not reproduced by any low-order
representation tested (Edgeworth in 16 future dims: single-cut gains <= ~5x and erratic).

## E6. Why controls and RQMC do not multiply (HEURISTIC; round 2 Q4)
Scrambled Sobol integrates the low-order ANOVA (and low-degree) components of the integrand with variance that decays
faster than 1/N; its residual error at moderate N is the high-order part. A control that removes the linear and
quadratic parts removes what RQMC already integrates, so G_R ~ G / (share of the RQMC error left) ~ 1 (dev d). Gains
multiply only for controls that remove high-order components, i.e. that capture the nonlinear future itself.

## E7. Why no layer-wise closure's window can grow along L = alpha n unless its error is o(1/n) (PROVEN arithmetic
## under measured laws; astra r1 Q6 gives the same criterion)
Let a deductive layer-wise method have error e(n) and cost C(n) passes. RQMC reaches error e at about 1/(G e) passes
with G ~ 1.2-2.2 roughly constant in n (MEASURED, arc-update), so the method's gain at its own cost is
G_RQMC ~ 1 / (G C(n) e(n)) and its window [C(n), 1/(G e(n))] has width ratio 1 / (G C e). Any method that carries a
covariance-level state through an n x n layer pays at least one n x n x n product per layer, C(n) >= c n passes.
Hence a growing window needs e(n) = o(1/n). Measured families: ARC's fixed orders (e(n) ~ const at fixed L/n; gain
falls like 1/n, as arc-update measured: G_2 = 8.1, 4.8, 3.9, 2.6); the radius-corrected closure (e ~ alpha/n: gain
constant); the exact-marginal future-aware family at k = n/4 (benchmark; dev n e(n) ~ 0.02, 0.03, 0.013 at n = 64, 128,
256) would give a roughly constant gain IF it could be carried at Theta(n) passes, but it is carried by particles
(~5 passes per particle per run) or needs a k = n/4-dim deterministic representation (E5).

## E10. What the "k features exact" projection removes, exactly (PROVEN [C]), and what the share law suggests
Whiten at the cut, z = S^{-1/2}(h - mu), and rotate so that the kept features are the first k coordinates, z = (z_1, z_2)
(possible because the projection depends only on span(A)). Under the true law P, z_1 and z_2 are uncorrelated with unit
covariance; under the cond_k projection Q_k, z_1 has the true marginal and z_2 ~ N(0, I) independently of z_1 (the
linear-Gaussian conditional with matched first two moments). Expand the future's reading in normalised Hermite
polynomials of z, g = sum_alpha g^_alpha He_alpha(z), alpha = (alpha_1, alpha_2). Then E_{Q_k}[He_alpha] =
E_P[He_{alpha_1}(z_1)] 1{alpha_2 = 0}, so
   int g d(Q_k - P) = - sum_{alpha: alpha_2 != 0, |alpha| >= 3} g^_alpha h_alpha(P),   h_alpha(P) = E_P He_alpha(z),
against - sum_{|alpha| >= 3} g^_alpha h_alpha(P) for the moment-matched Gaussian (k = 0). PROVEN (exact identity; mean
and covariance matching kill |alpha| <= 2; the series converges when dP/dG is in L^2(G), otherwise it is a formal
expansion and the statement holds for every polynomial reading; the split itself, z_Q = P_B z + eta with eta ~ N(0, P_perp)
independent, B = S^{1/2} A, is exact). The projection removes exactly the Hermite modes of the future that live
ENTIRELY in the kept span. For a generic future whose degree-d modes spread their indices over the directions, the
modes entirely inside a span holding a fraction p of the index mass are ~p^d of the energy, so removal would be tiny at
d >= 3; the measured law (single-cut improvement ~ (1 - s)^{-1.1}, s = captured share of the whitened nonlinear
Jacobian energy, corr 0.96 over 384 cells at n = 64, post hoc) is instead what a RIDGE-LIKE future gives (each
nonlinear mode concentrated on one relevance direction, so the removed fraction is ~s). HEURISTIC reading: the parts of
the future that couple to the cloud's non-Gaussianity act like sums of one-dimensional nonlinear functions of the
relevance directions; the number of such directions needed for a fixed share grows with the width (E4, astra r2 Q2).

## E12. A pricing bound for future-chosen "k features exact" projections (PROVEN UNDER a random-sign model) [C]
From E10, the single-cut defect of the cond projection on a k-dim span V is D_V = - sum_{alpha not inside V, |alpha| >= 3}
g^_alpha h_alpha(P) (normalised Hermite coefficients in a basis adapted to V). Model the cloud's higher Hermite moments as
h_alpha = eps_alpha mu_alpha with independent symmetric signs eps_alpha that are independent of V (true when V is computed
from the future and the cloud's first two moments only, as the futnl/futv/futmf2 subspaces essentially are) and fixed
magnitudes mu_alpha. Then E_eps |D_V|^2 = sum_{alpha not inside V} |g^_alpha|^2 mu_alpha^2 = sum_d (||H_d||^2 -
||P_V^{tensor d} H_d||^2), with H_d the degree-d tensor of the products g^_alpha mu_alpha. By astra r1's flattening bound,
||P_V^{tensor d} H_d||^2 <= (k / r_inf(H_d)) ||H_d||^2, so
   E_eps |D_V|^2 >= (1 - k / min_{d >= 3} r_inf(H_d)) E_eps |D_0|^2,
for every V of dimension k chosen independently of the signs, D_0 being the moment-matched Gaussian's defect. Proof: the
cross terms vanish by independence of the signs; the rest is the definition of P_V^{tensor d} in the adapted basis and
the flattening bound. QED. So a future-chosen projection can improve the expected single-cut defect at most by the
factor 1/(1 - k/r), r the smallest stable rank of the future-times-cloud coefficient tensors. The measured improvement
law (single-cut improvement ~ (1 - s)^{-1.1} in the captured nonlinear relevance share s, post hoc) is consistent with
this form when s ~ k/r. What the model leaves out: sign patterns correlated with V (a method that also reads the cloud's
higher moments may exploit cancellations, as in astra r1's adaptive-defect counterexample), and structured magnitudes
(the radius mode concentrates mu on specific alpha, which is why one direction, the mean direction, matters so much).
